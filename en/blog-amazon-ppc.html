Amazon PPC: how to steer your advertising toward profit

Campaign types, metrics and a worked example show how to connect advertising on Amazon with your margin.

November 27, 2026 · 6 min read · Mario Moosbauer

Amazon PPC (pay-per-click) is advertising on Amazon where you only pay when someone clicks your ad. Whether it pays off is decided by the contribution margin per product; ACoS alone cannot tell you. This guide covers the campaign types, the most important metrics and the approach we use to manage Amazon ad accounts.

growald is an AI agency specialising in e-commerce and Amazon. This text is for brands that already sell on Amazon and want their ad account managed professionally.

What is Amazon PPC and how is it billed?

With Amazon PPC you bid on search terms or products. If you win the auction, your ad appears in the search results or on product pages. You only pay when someone clicks.

Whether your ad is shown depends mainly on two things:

  • Your bid compared with other advertisers
  • how relevant your product is to the search term

What happens after the click is up to the listing. If your images, title and reviews are weak, you need more clicks per sale and pay more advertising per order. That is why a solid listing foundation comes before the first advertising euro.

Which Amazon PPC campaign types are there?

Three types matter for brands. They complement each other, so we plan them as one system.

Campaign typeWhere the ad appearsTypical job
Sponsored ProductsSearch results and product pages, promotes a single productSales and ranking of individual ASINs
Sponsored Brandsabove the search results, with logo and several productsBuild brand awareness, show the range
Sponsored Displayon and off Amazon, including on product pagesRetargeting and protecting your own product pages

Sponsored Products is the base for most accounts. Brands and Display build on it once the base runs profitably.

Which metrics matter in Amazon PPC?

These are the four metrics you should know:

MetricFormulaWhat it tells you
ACoSAd spend ÷ ad salesShare of ad spend in the sales the advertising brings in
ROASAd sales ÷ ad spendSales per advertising euro, the inverse of ACoS
TACoSAd spend ÷ total salesAdvertising share of total sales, including organic
Break-even ACoSContribution margin before advertising ÷ net selling priceIf ACoS is above it, you lose money on every sale

ACoS only tells you something about profit once you compare it with your margin. For a product with a 40% margin, an ACoS of 25% is very good; at a 20% margin it is a loss. There is more on this in the article ACoS alone is not enough.

What is left of an advertising euro? A worked example

All numbers in this section are a worked example, not customer data.

A product sells for €30.00 net. From that you deduct:

  • Purchasing: €8.00
  • Amazon fees: €4.50
  • Shipping and fulfillment: €5.50

That leaves €12.00 contribution margin before advertising, which is 40% of the net price. So the break-even ACoS is 40%.

At 25% ACoS, a sale costs €7.50 in advertising. That leaves €4.50 contribution margin after advertising, or 15% of the price. If at least 10% should be left, your target ACoS is 30%.

Now the whole account, in the same worked example:

  • Ad sales: €20,000
  • Ad spend: €5,000
  • ACoS: 25%, ROAS: 4.0
  • Total sales including organic sales: €50,000
  • TACoS: 10%

40% of the €20,000 in ad sales is €8,000 contribution margin. After €5,000 in ad spend, €3,000 is left. An account at 25% ACoS can look healthy overall and still contain individual products whose ACoS is above their break-even. Those products cost money, and only the calculation per product shows it.

Rule of thumb: Work out the break-even ACoS per ASIN before you change bids. The break-even ACoS calculator does this for each product separately. In our Amazon PPC management, this calculation comes before every bid change.

How do you manage Amazon PPC step by step?

This is how we approach an account. The order is deliberate: numbers first, then bids.

  1. Calculate the contribution margin per ASIN. Deduct purchasing, Amazon fees and shipping, and derive the break-even ACoS from that.
  2. Review the account. Look at campaign structure, budgets and overlaps, especially automatic and manual campaigns competing for the same search terms.
  3. Clean up the structure. Separate campaigns by goal and product phase: launch, growth, defense.
  4. Set a target ACoS per product. Each ASIN gets its own value, derived from its contribution margin.
  5. Manage bids and budgets. Adjust bids to the target ACoS, exclude search terms without sales, move strong terms into their own campaigns.
  6. Keep adjusting. Season, competition and price changes move the break-even, and the limits have to move with it.

Which mistakes make ad accounts expensive?

Five patterns keep showing up in ad accounts:

  • The goal is set on ACoS instead of contribution margin.
  • Automatic and manual campaigns bid on the same search terms.
  • Sponsored Products, Brands and Display run without joint planning.
  • New products launch without their own launch campaign and get hardly any data.
  • Reporting shows ACoS but no link to the actual margin.

The last point is the most expensive. Without the link to margin, every account looks successful as long as sales grow.

Frequently asked questions about Amazon PPC

What is a good ACoS?

That depends on your product's break-even ACoS. A product with a 40% contribution margin can carry more advertising than one with 20%.

How much budget do I need for Amazon PPC?

There is no fixed number. What matters is getting enough clicks to learn from the data. Our ongoing management starts at €1,500 net per month; the ad budget paid to Amazon comes on top.

How long does it take for Amazon PPC to work?

Think in weeks, not days. Bids can only be judged once enough clicks have come in.

Should I run Amazon PPC myself or outsource it?

Doing it yourself works as long as the account is small and someone on the team has the time. With a larger budget or several products, ongoing management pays off, because structure and search terms need constant upkeep.
