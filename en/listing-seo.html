When listings are neither found nor understood.

Many listings are either too technical or not aligned closely enough with search data.

  • Titles are either too narrow or overloaded with keywords
  • Bullet points describe features, but not the actual customer benefit
  • Backend keywords are not fully used or are duplicated
  • Listings are created once and never adjusted to new search data
  • Content barely differs from the competition

How Amazon reads a listing.

Whether a product shows up in a search depends on two things: how well the text matches the query and how well the product sells.

For the match, the title, bullet points and backend keywords matter most. On the sales side, sales figures, reviews and availability play a part, among other things. A good listing alone will not get you to position 1. It does make sure that ads and promotions lead to a page buyers understand.

Which parts of the listing you control

  • Title with brand, product type and the most important search term
  • Bullet points with a concrete customer benefit
  • Backend keywords for terms that have no place in the visible text
  • Images and A+ Content for the buying decision

The first three points are text, which is our core work. We plan images and A+ Content along with it, but produce them only by agreement.

What we check in a listing.

Every listing goes through the same checklist before we rework it. That way you see up front where the biggest lever is.

AreaQuestion we askTypical finding
TitleIs the most important search term near the front, and is the title still readable?Brand and filler words take up the start
Bullet pointsDoes each point explain a benefit rather than only a feature?A list of technical data
Backend keywordsDo they contain only terms that are not already in the title?Duplicates, wasted space
Search termsDoes the listing cover the terms customers actually search for?The brand's own wording instead of customer language

How to recognize a good Amazon SEO agency.

Many Amazon SEO agencies advertise with similar promises. These questions help you choose, whichever provider you talk to.

  1. What data is the copy based on, and do I get the keyword list?
  2. Who exactly writes the copy, and does that person know my category?
  3. How is it measured whether the rework had an effect, and after how long?
  4. What happens to the copy and the analyses if we end the collaboration?
  5. Is there a pricing model that starts without a long contract?
